Before you edit

Start with what
is true today.

Do not add suburbs just because you would like to rank there. Start with the places your business actually serves and can support.

This is a consistency check for your content. It does not promise visibility, rankings or enquiries.

See a worked example ↓
01

Confirm your operating coverage

Write down the suburbs or areas you currently serve. Note any differences by service and any genuine exclusions. Ask the owner to confirm the list before editing public information.

Check: can you explain the same coverage to a customer and to your publisher?

02

Review your Google Business Profile

Compare the public service areas with your confirmed list. Check relevant services and wording too. If you need to change a Google Business Profile service area, use Google’s current editing instructions and eligibility rules.

Google distinguishes service-area and hybrid businesses. A service-area business that does not serve customers at its address should remove its address from the profile. Do not use an address or service area to imply eligibility you do not have.

Read Google’s official service-area guidance ↗
03

Check the relevant website pages

Read the home page, service pages, coverage page, footer and any other place that names locations. Compare each mention with the confirmed list. Do not remove accurate information or rewrite content simply to make an edit.

Check: does any old suburb list or broad “all of Perth” statement need correction?

04

Follow the quote-request journey

Open the contact page and enquiry form. Check location choices, prompts, automated confirmations and any existing quote instructions. Can a customer tell what location information to send and whether you need to confirm availability?

Only request information you actually use. Do not add availability promises or response times the owner has not confirmed.

05

Assign changes, then check again

Record the location of each mismatch, the confirmed wording and who will publish it. Keep already-clear items unchanged. After publication, revisit the public pages and walk through the enquiry steps again.

Check: do the profile, website and enquiry information now tell a consistent story?

A fictional worked example

One coverage change.
A few places to check.

These are invented business details, not a client case or a recommendation about where to operate.

An owner confirms that their standard home-cleaning service now covers Maylands and Bayswater. The website still says “all of Perth”, while the quote form lists a suburb they no longer serve.

ILLUSTRATIVE REPLACEMENT

“Our standard home-cleaning service covers Maylands and Bayswater. Tell us your suburb when you request a quote so we can check whether your request fits our current service.”

  • Compare the Google profile’s service areas with the owner-confirmed coverage, using Google’s current rules.
  • Replace the inaccurate broad website statement where it appears.
  • Remove the outdated form choice and check the confirmation wording.
  • Keep a clear, accurate service-exclusion statement unchanged.
  • Ask the owner or web contact to publish, then recheck each public touchpoint.

Only use this wording if every detail is true for the business. A coverage edit does not guarantee acceptance of an individual quote request.
